## Confirmed P1 and repair

Two producer streams can stage distinct generations of one `(repo, revision)`.
Before this repair, g2 could become the ledger's current sealed identity while
g1 remained `Pending` or `Staged`. Retention could then reap g1, but even when
g1 was retained its activation failed because activation requires the *current*
sealed track identity. The source reservation remained unresolved. The retained
pre-fix daemon reproduced this: g1 activation returned `NOT_READY`, observing
g2 as current. See `l2-proof/reaudit-process-fixed-20260927.json` (2 passed,
1 failed; the two existing process tests passed).

The authority retention planner now refuses a candidate newer than an
unresolved source target for the same pair. A failed g2 publication may leave
physical staged/sealed files, but it cannot record g2 authority or advance the
ledger; its original journal and reservation are retried after g1 activates.
Unresolved targets are also required in the retained set. The catalog rejects
activation that would overtake a lower unresolved target and refuses a *new*
event targeting a generation at or below the active head. Existing exact-event
replays still resolve their original binding.

This is deliberate backpressure. An unresolvable older source event requires
operator recovery of its original journal/publication; there is no automatic
reservation eviction. Pre-existing roots already overtaken before this repair
are not migrated or silently pronounced healthy.
The repository envelope also has an explicit 8192-event cap and no automatic
identity eviction; exhaustion refuses new publications. Producer-supplied
source-byte hashes remain attestations until the external producer is verified.
